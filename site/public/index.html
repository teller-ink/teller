The table plays.teller keeps the books.

A companion for in-person tabletop RPGs. Not a virtual tabletop — the opposite of one. Dice, minis and rulings stay on the table. Initiative, hit points, conditions and fog go on the screens around it.

$brew install teller-ink/tap/teller

Free and open source. Runs on your own machine.

The thesis

Anything you touch stays physical. Anything that's bookkeeping goes on glass.

Stays on the table

  • Dicerolled by hand and read aloud
  • Minismoved by whoever's turn it is
  • Terrainbuilt, not rendered
  • Rulingsmade by the humans in the room

Goes on glass

  • Initiativewho's up and who's on deck
  • Hit pointsand anything else your game counts: ammo, slots, luck
  • Conditionson, off, and how bad
  • Maps and fogat true scale, where one drawn inch is one real inch
  • Handoutsfullscreen, when the GM says
  • Rulebooksyour own PDFs, searchable mid-game

The humans at the table are the rules engine.

teller may roll dice, measure a range or work out a default. Every result lands somewhere a human can type over, and the stored value always wins.

How a table works

One address. Every screen in the room opens it.

  1. One machine hosts

    A laptop, a Pi, anything that runs Node. Type teller host and it prints an address.

  2. Every screen opens that address

    Phones, tablets, the TV under the minis. Each one shows a short pairing code. Nothing is installed on any of them.

  3. The GM adopts them

    Type a screen's code into the console and say what that screen is. Change your mind whenever you like.

teller host
  teller

  data      ~/.teller
  key file  ~/.teller/dm.key

  open on this machine:
    http://localhost:4526

  other screens in the room:
    http://192.168.1.20:4526

  ctrl-c to stop
  • No accounts.
  • No installs on anyone's device.
  • No cloud in the play path.
  • A table with no internet works completely.

Screens

Six things a screen can be.

What a screen is, is an assignment the GM makes and can change at will.

  • console

    The GM's seat of power: roster, encounter runner, boards, books. Split it one pane per screen and it becomes a digital GM screen.

  • table

    The shared display under the minis. The active map, full bleed, at true physical scale on a calibrated screen. Nothing else.

  • seat

    One player's own card: sheet, gear, dice and self-serve counters. Runs on a phone, a tablet, or a mounted touch bar.

  • board

    A player-facing companion: turn order, public state, the GM's notices. Player-safe by construction.

  • art

    A fullscreen frame for the current handout.

  • badge

    An outward-facing nameplate for each player.

Passive screens never grow buttons. Everything they show arrives from the console, live.

The house rules

What teller will always do, and what it never will.

Override is the architecture

teller may roll dice, measure ranges and derive defaults. Every result lands somewhere a human can type over. It never decides something nobody can change.

Every change is logged

And undo walks it back: a deployed encounter, a deletion, a turn, a roll. All of it, coherently.

One secret

The GM's key, minted on first run. Everything else is an assignment the host checks. A seat may edit its one character and nothing else. Passive screens get a redacted snapshot, with hidden things actually stripped.

Your data is a folder

A campaign is one SQLite file. Back up ~/.teller, or carry it on a stick.

Ships empty

No game, no vocabulary, nobody's book.

A character is named lists of named entries. What a list means is a declaration a system ships. Content is files on your shelf, installed by dropping a folder in.

A system
How one game works: its dice, its distance bands, its conditions, its screens. Pure mechanics, freely shareable.
A pack
What exists in a world: bestiary, items, prose, art and the branded look, layered over the system.
A story
One table's campaign, exported as a single .story file that carries only what you wrote.
A plugin
Extra function at declared seams, with needs you approve once. An assistant that proposes a monster's turn, a store, whatever you build. teller ships with zero.
  1. teller's floor
  2. the system
  3. packs
  4. the campaign
  5. the table's own files

Every layer merges, and later wins. Restate one name in a file and it's yours.

Rulebook PDFs attach by content hash and get full-text search. Your book, on your host, one download.

Hardware

If you're that kind of table.

Nothing needs special hardware. Phones and a TV do everything. But teller is built to be worth hardware: a TV under a sheet of acrylic becomes a calibrated battlemat, and a touch bar at each seat becomes a player's console.

Install

Put it on the table.

$brew install teller-ink/tap/teller
$teller hostprints the address every screen opens
$teller keythe one secret; the GM's device unlocks with it
